# Poblysh Article Revision Checklist

Use this after a first draft and before final approval.

The goal is not merely to make the essay correct. The goal is to make it
distilled, memorable, and structurally inevitable.

## 1. Thesis And Entry

- Is the central argument strong by sentence 2?
- Does the subtitle make sense on first read without needing the essay to
  decode it?
- Does the subtitle promise one specific hidden cost, tension, or reframe?
- Does the subtitle complement the title instead of paraphrasing it?
- Does the opening preserve mystery before explaining too much?
- Does the first paragraph create pressure rather than simply briefing the
  reader?
- Is the title intelligible on first read as well as intellectually interesting?

Subtitle automatic fails:
- it could fit several essays with almost no change
- it uses abstraction the opening does not immediately ground
- it sounds atmospheric but does not promise a reward for reading
- it reads like a summary line instead of an invitation into tension

## 2. Abstract Term Pressure

When a term carries repeated conceptual weight, test it:

- Have we defined it through scenes, signals, or consequences?
- Could a smart reader explain what makes it observable in company life?
- Are we repeating the term because it is useful or because it is covering
  missing mechanics?

Common risk terms:
- legibility
- seriousness
- readiness
- perception
- witness
- public meaning

## 3. Mechanics

- Does the essay show how the problem works, not just that it exists?
- If the piece names a burden in sales, hiring, partnerships, or fundraising,
  does it show the mechanism clearly?
- Could an operator point to where this shows up inside actual company life?

## 4. Objection Strength

- Does the essay treat founder resistance as rational?
- Does it acknowledge the strongest case against the argument, not only the
  easiest one?
- Does it admit when narration should wait, when premature legibility can
  damage the work, or when silence may still be strategically correct?

## 5. Scope Check

- Is the current framing too narrow for the deeper idea?
- Does the essay accidentally confuse one instance of the problem for the whole
  problem?
- Is there a more durable frame under the current frame, such as translation
  under return, or witness under visibility?

## 6. Reference Discipline

- Does each literary, historical, political, or religious reference feel
  inevitable rather than inserted?
- Has the essay prepared enough runway for the reference to land naturally?
- Is the reference doing analytical work, or merely displaying range?
- Would the paragraph be stronger in the essay's own voice?

## 7. Sentence Rhythm

- Are too many sentences long, balanced, and equally compressed?
- Do we have shorter statement lines that reset pace?
- Is the piece readable on a phone without losing force?
- Are there 2 to 4 lines that could stand alone on a statement page?

## 8. Density Balance

- Does every paragraph operate at the same level of seriousness and abstraction?
- Have we mixed dense interpretive lines with cleaner declarative ones?
- Are there places where one simple sentence would hit harder than one elegant
  paragraph?

## 9. Month Logic

- Is the month shaping the essay without being over-announced?
- Does the month appear as pressure, memory, ritual, reporting cycle, or
  atmosphere rather than as repeated calendar labeling?
- Would the piece still be interesting if the month name were removed from the
  prose?

## 10. Residue

- Does the ending leave a line behind, not merely a conclusion?
- Does the last paragraph feel inevitable rather than tidy?
- Does the reader leave with one sharper distinction or phrase?

## 11. Final Distillation Test

Ask these questions directly:

- Is this merely intelligent, or is it distilled?
- Is this merely correct, or is it memorable?
- Is this essay saying something only Poblysh would say?
- What is the strongest critique of the piece, and has the draft already
  answered it?
