# Poblysh Journal Editorial Action Typology

Poblysh Journal treats editorial action as the job an essay performs on a startup signal, not the layout it eventually uses.

## Core questions

Before choosing an action, ask:
- Are we mapping a system of visibility or trust?
- Are we reading one artifact, phrase, or public signal closely?
- Are we correcting a shallow market assumption?
- Are we tracing how a positioning logic developed over time?
- Are we leaving the reader with a strong house judgment?

## Core action groups

### Systems and incentives
- `anatomy`: map how a visibility or perception system works
- `cultural-diagnosis`: treat a company symptom as evidence of a deeper market habit
- `pattern-recognition`: name an emerging recurring pattern in operator life
- `misreading-correction`: replace a bad reading with a sharper one

### Reading and interpretation
- `close-read`: unpack one phrase, sentence, artifact, or signal
- `craft-study`: study how a narrative asset or communication system is built
- `dissection`: break down a launch, announcement, or update piece by piece
- `comparison`: place two signals or companies in tension so each clarifies the other

### Time and accumulation
- `memory-piece`: use remembered operator experience to open a larger insight
- `origin-story`: recover the beginning of a pattern, posture, or trust signal
- `genealogy`: trace how a public meaning evolved through stages
- `excavation`: recover a useful but neglected signal or milestone

### Voice and declaration
- `verdict`: give a clean house judgment
- `portrait`: show how one operator or company embodies a wider argument
- `sensory-report`: write a field-note style piece from a live setting or decision moment
- `manifesto-piece`: state and defend a principle of Poblysh's worldview

### Narrative pressure
- `character-fiction`: use a composite operator vignette to dramatize a tension
- `mythic-retelling`: reinterpret a startup cliché or market myth through a new frame

## Selection rule

Choose the action that makes the argument most legible to a founder, CMO, PMM, or strategic operator.
