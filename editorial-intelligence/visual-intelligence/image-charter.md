# Poblysh Journal Image Charter

## Purpose

This charter defines the editorial role of images in The Poblysh Journal.

It exists to keep imagery from slipping into decoration, generic atmosphere, or
taste-signaling without consequence.

The audience for this document is:
- editors
- sequence planners
- illustrators
- prompt writers

## Core principle

Images in Poblysh do not exist to make the sequence prettier.

They exist to make an abstract strategic condition feel real enough that the
reader pauses, recognizes it, and carries its pressure forward into the next
page.

Text names the idea.
Typography gives it pace.
Imagery gives it emotional weight.

## What an image must do

Every image should do at least one of these jobs:

### 1. Threshold

Stage the contradiction before the essay explains it.

Use when the reader needs to feel a condition before they can name it.

### 2. Trace

Make public residue visible:
- paperwork
- surfaces
- rooms
- objects
- preserved evidence
- institutional remains

Use when the essay is about what the market can still find, still read, or
still mistake for the company.

### 3. Hinge

Hold the reader at the exact point where the argument changes state.

Use when a page turn needs more than a sentence. The image should absorb the
shock, gravity, or withheld realization of the turn.

### 4. Translation

Make the labor of interpretation feel human.

Use when the argument moves from company motion to the work of making that
motion legible. Favor posture, hands, marked notes, and editorial fragments
over literal “teamwork” scenes.

### 5. Residue

Leave an afterimage the sentence alone cannot leave behind.

Use when the sequence needs a lingering emotional condition:
- quiet expense
- institutional seriousness
- withheld motion
- stale evidence
- delayed recognition

## What images must not do

Images should not:
- decorate a page that is already complete without them
- summarize the paragraph literally
- behave like generic startup mood boards
- prove that Poblysh has taste
- provide filler breathing room with no argumentative role
- imitate ad creative, campaign art, or polished SaaS brand visuals

If the image only says “this is calm” or “this is editorial,” it is not yet
earning its place.

## The emotional standard

Poblysh images should create one of these reading effects:
- pause
- recognition
- unease
- gravity
- reflective stillness
- sharpened attention

The reader should feel that something has become more legible, not merely more
beautiful.

## The house lens

Poblysh writes about:
- visibility as infrastructure
- trust as accumulated legibility
- timing as an operating layer
- public meaning as market residue

That means images should usually help us feel one of these conditions:
- motion that has not yet been publicly recognized
- evidence that survived while newer reality stayed private
- the cost of interpretive lag
- the work of translation between private clarity and public meaning
- the institutional weight of being re-read correctly or incorrectly

## Approved image families

These families remain the most useful because they can carry pressure without
becoming literal:

### Spatial / Environmental

Good for:
- stillness
- thresholds
- withheld motion
- institutional atmosphere with consequence

### Object-Based Symbolic

Good for:
- documentation
- compliance
- proof
- preserved traces
- operational seriousness

### Human Editorial Fragment

Good for:
- translation
- judgment
- authorship
- burden
- restraint

## Page-role guidance

### Full-Bleed Silence

Not “a beautiful pause.”

It should hold a condition in place long enough for the reader to feel its
weight.

### Detail Study

Not “texture.”

It should show residue, proof, or institutional consequence.

### Bridge Page

Not “one nice image and one line.”

It should transfer the reader from one state of understanding to another.

### Image-Only Page

Not “atmosphere with no burden.”

It should carry symbolic or emotional burden even when no text appears.

## The removal test

Before keeping an image page, ask:

1. What invisible condition does this image make legible?
2. What exact emotional pressure should the reader feel here?
3. What turn in the argument does it deepen or hold?
4. What would be lost if this became a text-only page?

If the answer is vague, the image is decorative and should be cut or replaced.

## Prompting rule

Prompt for condition before subject.

Do not begin from:
- corridor
- notebook
- meeting room
- papers

Begin from:
- stale evidence still governing perception
- a company changed in private but not yet re-read in public
- documentation carrying more truth than the homepage
- a room holding back speech because interpretation is not ready yet

Then choose the subject that can carry that condition.

## Example reframing

Weak:

`A quiet fintech corridor before the day begins.`

Stronger:

`A corridor in a financial company that has changed faster than its public story, one lit doorway and long still surfaces, institutional calm with hidden motion, directional morning light, restrained monochrome palette, vertical 5:8 framing.`

Weak:

`Printed licensing documents on a desk.`

Stronger:

`Licensing paperwork as the residue of a company becoming more regulated and substantial before the market updates its reading, marked notes, face-down phone, dark table, tactile paper texture, serious reflective mood, vertical 5:8 framing.`

## Decision rule

The image is successful when it does not merely match the tone of the essay.

It should make the essay harder to forget.
